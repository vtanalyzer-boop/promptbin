Photos with two people are harder to edit than solo portraits, because the model has to keep two faces consistent at the same time. A reliable couple prompt ChatGPT can follow is specific about both people, the shared scene and what must not change. This guide gives you the rules, then copy-ready prompts for four popular moods.

## Why couple edits go wrong

Most failed edits have the same cause: the prompt only says "my face". With two people, ChatGPT may keep one face and quietly redraw the other. A strong couple prompt ChatGPT users can rely on repeats the identity lock for both people and keeps the pose and clothing unchanged unless you want a change.

## Five rules for every couple prompt

- **Name both people.** Write "keep both faces unchanged", not "keep my face".
- **Describe the shared scene in one sentence.** Beach at sunset, rainy city street, string lights.
- **Keep poses and clothes the same** unless the edit is about outfits.
- **Pick a single mood**, such as romantic, playful or calm.
- **Name the crop.** Vertical 4:5 or 9:16 suits phones best.

## How to fix common couple problems

- **One face changed:** reply with "restore the original face of the person on the left, keep everything else".
- **Heights or proportions look off:** add "keep body proportions and relative height unchanged".
- **An umbrella or prop hides a face:** add "make sure neither face is covered".
- **The mood feels too heavy:** ask for "softer contrast and warmer tones".

## Start with the right photo

A couple prompt ChatGPT can follow still needs a decent source. Choose a picture where both faces are clearly visible, roughly the same size and not cut by the frame. If one person is turned away or half hidden, the model has to guess, and guesses are where faces drift.

Group shots, sunglasses and heavy shadows make it harder. If that's your only photo, lower your expectations and plan for one or two correction rounds.

## A worked example: two messages, not one

Trying to change the scene, the outfits and the mood in a single request is the quickest way to lose a face. Split it. In the first message, change only the setting. In the second, adjust the finish.

**Message one**

> Move this couple into a cozy cafe with warm lamps, a wooden table and a blurred window behind them. Keep both faces, hairstyles, outfits and poses exactly the same. Natural light, realistic, vertical 4:5.

**Message two**, once the faces look right:

> Keep everything the same, but add soft film grain and slightly warmer colors. Do not change either face.

## Decision rules for tricky couple photos

- **If the faces look slightly off:** shorten the prompt. Long scene descriptions compete with the identity lock.
- **If one person looks older or younger:** add "keep both people's apparent age and skin tone unchanged".
- **If the hands or an embrace look strange:** ask for a calmer pose only if you're happy to lose the original pose, otherwise ask ChatGPT to redraw just the hands.
- **If the background steals the scene:** write "background softly blurred, couple clearly in focus".
- **If you keep getting the same bad result:** try a different photo before you rewrite the prompt.

## Be honest about the limits

Even a well-built couple prompt won't give you a perfect likeness every time. Results vary by photo and ChatGPT version. Pets, children and extra people in the background add more chances for errors. If you only need a nicer background, the Remove people in the background card can clean the frame first.

Only edit photos of people who are comfortable with it, and think before you post a stranger's face anywhere. It's also worth keeping the original, since an edit that looks right on your phone can show small flaws when enlarged.
