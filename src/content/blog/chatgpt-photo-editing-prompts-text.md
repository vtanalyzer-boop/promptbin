Adding words to a photo is one of the most requested edits: a birthday name, a poster headline, a quote in handwriting, a travel title. It is also where results go wrong most often, because lettering is harder for an image model than lighting or color. This guide shows how to write ChatGPT photo editing prompts text that gets the wording, the style and the position right.

## The four things a text prompt must say

Every reliable set of ChatGPT photo editing prompts text includes the same four details:

1. **The exact words**, in quotation marks, spelled the way you want them.
2. **The lettering style**: bold sans-serif, elegant handwriting, rounded playful letters.
3. **The position**: top, lower third, centered, along the left edge.
4. **A protection rule**: the text must never cover the face or the main subject.

If you skip any one of these, ChatGPT will choose for you, and the choice is rarely what you pictured.

## Tips to avoid spelling mistakes

- **Keep text short.** Three to six words is far safer than a full sentence.
- **Use capital letters for headlines.** They are easier to render cleanly.
- **Spell tricky names out.** If a name has an unusual spelling, add "spelled exactly as written".
- **Check every letter** before you post. If one letter is wrong, ask ChatGPT to fix only that word.
- **Avoid tiny text.** Small print often turns into unreadable shapes. Ask for a larger size instead.

## When the text covers a face

Add "keep faces and hands completely clear of text" and name a safe zone, such as "place all text in the top fifth of the image". If the problem persists, crop the photo so there is more empty space where the words will go.

## Where to put the words for each kind of photo

Position is the part people forget, and it changes how the whole picture feels. A few rules of thumb that work for most ChatGPT AI photo editing prompts with words:

- **Close portrait, face fills the frame.** Put text on the top or bottom edge only, and ask ChatGPT to add a small color band if the background is busy.
- **Full-body or wide shot.** Use the empty sky, wall or floor area. Name it: "in the empty space above the head."
- **Two people.** Center the text below both of them, or along a side with space. Our [couple prompt ChatGPT guide](/blog/chatgpt-couple-photo-prompts/) covers keeping both faces clear.
- **Busy background.** Ask for a soft dark gradient behind white text, or a light one behind dark text.

## Before and after: fixing a weak text prompt

Weak:

> Add a cool caption to my photo.

ChatGPT has to invent the words, the font and the place. Better:

> Add the caption "SUNDAY SLOW" in thin white capital letters across the bottom fifth of my photo, with a soft dark gradient behind it so it stays readable. Keep my face and the rest of the picture unchanged. Do not add any other text.

The last sentence matters. Without it, image models sometimes add extra words, stray symbols or a fake watermark.

## Worked example: a three-line poster

Say you want a name, a date and a short tagline. Don't stack all of that into one sentence. Describe each line on its own.

> Add three lines of text to my photo. Line one, large and bold: "MAYA". Line two, smaller: "JUNE 2026". Line three, smallest, in italic: "Golden hour". Center all three in the lower third, white letters, nothing covering my face.

Check each line separately when the image comes back. If the date is wrong, say so: "Fix only line two so it reads JUNE 2026." Don't regenerate everything.

## Common text failures and what to do

- **Letters are swapped or doubled.** Shorten the text, use capitals, and spell it out again in the follow-up.
- **Extra words appear.** Add "no other text anywhere in the image."
- **The font looks wrong.** Describe the shape rather than a font name: "thick rounded letters" or "thin elegant script."
- **Text looks pasted on.** Ask it to match the lighting: "text should look printed on the photo, with the same light direction."

Be honest with yourself about limits. Even with perfect wording, you may get a misspelled word on the first try.
